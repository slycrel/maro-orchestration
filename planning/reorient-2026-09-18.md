## The methodology gap (the real one)

> "This is ultimately on me. I'm trying to outsource software methodology that you
> know, but asking you to do what I want without being explicit about it; assuming
> capability that is probably possible, but unprompted, sort of an opposite
> dichotomy of a prompt that says 'make no mistakes' or maybe 'you're a top 10
> world class software architect'… it's not specific enough and unhelpful of an
> assumption, without the constraints needed for finding success in the sea of
> infinite possibilities. That darn taste and judgement thing again."
> — Jeremy, 2026-09-18

It is not only on him. Both halves are real, and they compound:

- **His half:** the constraints that would make architectural judgement
  applicable were held in his head and asked for implicitly.
- **The agent's half:** the methodology *was* available and went unapplied,
  because the visible next action is always at the layer with code in it. An
  agent optimising for the next concrete step will reliably pick depth over
  breadth unless something in the environment says otherwise.

**The fix is not "write better prompts."** It is that the constraints get
written down ONCE, in a place both sides read on entry, so applying them stops
depending on Jeremy remembering to say them. That is precisely what
`GOAL_BRAIN_GOPORT.md` was for — and it was stranded. The failure and its
intended remedy have the same root.

## The layer map (the new material; boundaries NOT ratified)

Derived 2026-09-18 from the actual packages and record kinds, as a way to make
"which bite is this?" answerable. **Jeremy has not signed off on these
boundaries** — specifically whether Intent and Decomposition are one layer or
two, and whether Adaptation is a layer at all or a responsibility inside
Decomposition.

| # | layer | the question it answers | packages | state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Ledger | what is durably true and re-derivable? | record, journal, thought, contracts | solid |
| 1 | **Intent** | what does the operator actually want? | run (intent, ask, ground) | **runs once, at the front** |
| 2 | Decomposition | how will we get there? | run (agenda, fork, gate), learn | static list + `after:` edges, no conditionals |
| 3 | Execution | do it, and record what was done | invoke, process, sheriff, supervise | solid |
| 4 | Observation | what actually happened, deterministically? | regression, invoke (evidence), verdict | growing |
| 5 | Judgment | what does it mean? *(model opinion)* | judgment | the 2026-09-17/18 arc |
| 6 | Resolution | given every reading, what is settled? | verdict | built; wired at closure ONLY |
| 7 | **Adaptation** | therefore what? | **none** | **the hole** |
| 8 | Delivery | get it to the operator, provably | run, tail, projector | solid |
| 9 | Learning | carry it forward | learn, experiment, pack | partial |

**Jeremy's two standing examples are not judgment problems**, which is why
working at Layer 5 never resolved them:

- **"Asked for a picture of a rabbit, got Bugs Bunny"** — technically true, not
  correct. **Layer 1.** The spec was satisfied; nothing ever captured *what
  would make the answer wrong*. The Layer 5 judge is handed that spec and
  correctly says "yes, a rabbit." No Layer 5 improvement fixes it. Latent
  mechanism worth pulling on: the closure question already asks for
  **falsifiers**; eliciting them at intent time and carrying them into the
  judge's state is the concrete shape of a fix, and it is small.
- **"Learning a language to draw a kanji"** — the task reveals the plan was
  wrong. **Layer 7.** A discovery mid-plan cannot reach the plan. Today it is
  either self-reported by the executor (`$MARO_ASK`, the lowest-standing signal
  in the engine's own doctrine) or it kills the attempt. The silent middle case
  — executor quietly produces a mediocre kanji, judge says "done", plan runs on
  — loses the discovery entirely, because nothing ever asks for it.

Detail, including the multi-question judge sketch and what must be vetted
first, is in `planning/feature-judgment-providers.md`, final section.
